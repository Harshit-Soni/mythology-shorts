**Already read in the critical edition (2026-11-25, for S01E08's tease):**
- Krishna: *reap the fruit of this deed; for three thousand years you will wander this earth*, with no one to speak to, alone, through empty lands, sick, smelling of pus and blood (10.16.9–12).
- The unborn child Ashwatthama's weapon struck will live and **become King Parikshit**, and rule for sixty years (10.16.3–4, 13–15). Vyasa confirms Krishna's words (10.16.16–17). Ashwatthama gives up his jewel and goes to the forest (10.16.18–19).

**A ready-made link to S1:** the unborn child Ashwatthama tried to kill is **Parikshit**, the king of S01E07. Krishna's curse is the same moment he promises that baby a life.

**To research (Stage 1), all from the critical edition first:**
- The night raid on the sleeping camp: Dhrishtadyumna, Draupadi's five sons, Shikhandi (Sauptika 10.8). **Sensitive: keep it symbolic.**
- Why he did it: his father Drona's death, and the owl attacking the sleeping crows that gave him the idea (10.1).
- The Brahmastras: Arjuna's and his, Vyasa and Narada stepping between them, Arjuna withdrawing his and Ashwatthama unable to, turning it on the wombs of the Pandava women (10.13–15).
- The jewel on his forehead: what the text says about it, and how he gave it up (10.16.18–19, and 10.11–12 for Draupadi's demand).
- **Pop-culture versions to keep apart:** "cursed forever", "still alive today", a forehead wound that never heals and oozes, sightings at forts and temples. The epic says **three thousand years**: that correction is itself a strong hook (catalogue, "Often told as Mahabharata, but not in it").
- Krishna's tone: this is the one curse in S1–S2 spoken by Krishna himself. Keep him just, not vengeful.

**Hook ideas (to check against the research):**
1. *Krishna cursed only one man. He's said to be still walking the earth.* (Check "only one" before using it.)
2. *Everyone says Ashwatthama is immortal. The Mahabharata gives a number: three thousand years.*
3. *He tried to kill a baby before it was born. That baby became King Parikshit.*
