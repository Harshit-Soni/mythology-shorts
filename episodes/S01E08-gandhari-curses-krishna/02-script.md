# Script: S01E08 v2, The Mother Who Cursed Krishna (season finale)

**v2 (2026-11-25):** one plain story. v1 is in `review/02-script-v1.md`. The owner's note on v1: super confusing, not clear.

**What was wrong, and what changed:**
- **Who is who was never said.** Now beat 2 says who fought, beat 3 who lost, beat 4 whose side Krishna was on.
- **Two blames fought each other** ("my own son caused this", then "you could have stopped this"). Sparing the Pandavas is cut.
- **Cryptic lines are gone:** "only I can end my clan", "the omens of war returned", "to make her words true", "looked on".
- **The curse is said once, in plain parts,** and the second half ticks them off in the same order: the family, the thirty-six years, alone in a forest.
- **The season look-back is cut.** The story ends where it ends, then one line for next season.

**Spine:** a mother loses all her sons → she blames Krishna → she curses him → he accepts → thirty-six years later each part comes true → he's kind to the man who shot him.
**Opening:** the series sting (**शाप VIII / CURSED VIII**, ≈1 s).
**Length:** EN 210 words ≈ 82 s, HI 226 words ≈ 89 s, both with the sting, at E07's measured rates (2.6 / 2.58 words/s).
**Source used:** the critical edition, Stri Parva 11.25 and Mausala Parva 16.3–5; Ganguli agrees. **The research rows are not yet verified by the owner.**
**Source on screen:** "Mahabharata, Stri & Mausala Parva" / "महाभारत, स्त्री और मौसल पर्व".

## Accuracy notes
- **Beat 1:** "every word": each part, see the table. The wretched means and the weeping women (11.25.41–42) are left out, and came true too (16.5.10, 20).
- **Beat 2:** cousins, 1.103–108. HI *भाइयों* (cousin-brothers) is natural Hindi.
- **Beat 3:** a hundred sons, 1.107.37; her daughter lives. She and the Pandavas' meeting (11.13–15) is cut.
- **Beat 4:** "helped the Pandavas win": 9.62.15–19. The question shortens 11.25.36–37 ("why did you look on… you were able").
- **Beat 5:** 5.91, 5.93; her pleading with her son, 5.127, is for the replies.
- **Beat 6:** 11.25.38–41. "In thirty-six years": *when the thirty-sixth year comes*. **"Alone"** = with kin, ministers and sons dead, roaming the forest (*hatajñātiḥ… vanecaraḥ*); the word itself isn't in the verse. "You'll kill your own" (*jñātīn vadhiṣyasi*) is softened to "yours will too".
- **Beat 7:** 11.25.43–45, "as if slightly smiling… I know this too… the Yadavas will fall by one another's hands". His stern words after (11.26) are for the replies.
- **Beat 8:** Satyaki fought for the Pandavas, Kritavarma for the Kauravas (16.4.16–27). Their drinking is left out.
- **Beat 9:** 16.4.36–44. "Almost no one": Daruka, Babhru, Balarama and a few others outlive the fight. **Krishna first tried to stop it, and struck when his son fell (16.4.28, 34–35): for the replies.** **The epic's main cause is the sages' curse of the iron pestle (16.2): for the replies.** The script never says hers was the only cause.
- **Beat 10:** 16.5.16–18. In the text he remembers her when already in the forest; the order is eased. Balarama's end is left out.
- **Beat 11:** 16.5.19–20. The arrow struck his foot; not said.
- **Beat 12:** 16.5.20–21. "The curse was complete" is the narrator's.
- **Beat 13:** *āśvāsayat* (comforted), then he rose filling heaven and earth with splendour, and the gods met him (16.5.21–25). "Didn't punish": the text has no curse or punishment.
- **Beat 14:** CE 10.16.9–12. Say three thousand years, never "forever", if a number is ever used. Ashwatthama is Claude's pick, to be confirmed **before voicing**.

## Sensitivity
- **Krishna** is calm and unhurt in every picture: no wound, no blood, no body. Never "killed", "defeated". ("Die" is only in her curse, EN.)
- **Gandhari** is broken by grief, never bitter.
- **The field and the shore:** lamps, wheels, silhouettes. No bodies, no drinking.
- **The hunter** is an ordinary, horrified man.
