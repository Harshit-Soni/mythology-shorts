# S01E08: The Mother Who Cursed Krishna

- **Series:** S01 Shraap: Curses That Shaped the Mahabharata (episode 8, **season finale**)
- **Idea (one line):** The war is over and Gandhari's hundred sons are dead. She holds back her curse from the men who killed them. Then, on the battlefield, she turns to the one who could have stopped it, and curses Krishna: in the thirty-sixth year you'll lose your own clan the same way, and die alone in the forest. He smiles, and lets it come true.
- **Why it will work:**
  - The hook is already teased in E07: a mother curses Krishna himself, **and he accepts it**.
  - Everyone knows the war; few know that its last death is Krishna's, or that he remembered her words on the day.
  - The curse is an exact mirror: *you looked on while my family killed each other; yours will do the same.* Thirty-six years later he looks on.
  - It closes the season: after a season of curses on men, kings and a god, the last falls on Krishna, and not even he sets it aside.
- **Created:** 2026-11-25

## Angle (from the critical edition, 11.13–16, 11.25–26, 16.1–5, read 2026-11-25; research still to be done properly)
- **One spine:** she spares the killers → she asks Krishna *why did you look on?* → the curse → his smile → thirty-six years → he sees the signs, remembers her, and doesn't stop it → the forest, the arrow, forgiveness.
- **Hook options:**
  1. *A mother cursed Krishna. He smiled… and made it come true.*
  2. *She forgave the men who killed her hundred sons. She cursed Krishna.* ("Forgave": check; the text says her anger passed and she comforted them like a mother, 11.15.8.)
  3. *Krishna knew the year he would die. A mother had told him.*
- **The turn:** his answer, "as if slightly smiling" (11.25.43–45): no one but I can end the Yadavas; I know this already.
- **The promise kept:** in the thirty-sixth year he sees the omens, remembers Gandhari's words, and "wishing to make them true" sends his people to the sea (16.3.18–21).
- **The mirror:** her charge is *upekṣitāḥ*, "you looked on" (11.25.36–40). At Prabhasa, "knowing the turn of time", he doesn't hold them back (16.4.11) and stands leaning on the pestle as they fall (16.4.42). **But he isn't only a watcher:** he first runs in to stop it (16.4.28), and when his sons die he strikes too (16.4.35, 44).
- **Found in research (the thread):** Krishna went as envoy so that no one could say *he was able, yet didn't stop them* (5.91.16). That is exactly Gandhari's charge (11.25.36–38), and Vyasa uses the same words at the end (16.9.26).
- **The end:** lying in yoga, taken for a deer, hit in the sole; the hunter falls at his feet and Krishna comforts him, rising in light (16.5.19–21). A finale that ends on forgiveness.
- **Length:** ≤ 170 words in each language (75–80 s). Fewer events, each with a stated reason.
- **Clarity check before voicing:** who Gandhari is (blindfolded queen, mother of the hundred Kauravas), why Krishna could have stopped it, what the curse says, how each part comes true.

## Watch for in research
- **Two curses end the Yadavas:** Gandhari's (11.25) and the sages' on Samba, with the iron pestle (16.2.4–11). The Mausala Parva itself joins them (16.3.18–21, 16.5.17). The pestle is a story of its own: keep it out, or to a few words (the grass that turned to iron, 16.4.34–39, comes from it).
- **Krishna's harsh reply** (11.26.1–5): he tells her the fault is hers. It makes her less simple: should it be in the narration, or in the replies? At the least, don't make him merely gentle.
- **She nearly cursed the Pandavas first:** Vyasa stopped her (11.13.2–11), reminding her of her own words, "where dharma is, there is victory". Her glance under the cloth marks Yudhishthira's nails (11.15.6–7).
- **How she sees the field:** by divine sight, Vyasa's gift (11.16.1–4). Does she stay blindfolded?
- **What power the curse has:** tapas from serving her husband (11.25.39). Exactly what she says: the thirty-sixth year; kinsmen, ministers and sons dead; wandering in the forest; a wretched death; his women falling as hers do.
- **"Hundred sons":** check the count in these chapters, and Yuyutsu (not hers).
- **Durvasa's payasa** (16.5.17) is the epic's other reason for the sole of the foot. Pop versions (Vali reborn as Jara, the jewel-like toe): check, and keep out.
- **Jara:** in 16.2.10 the sages name *jarā* ("old age") as what will pierce him; in 16.5.19 Jara is the hunter.
- **Krishna's death:** what the text says about him rising to his own place (16.5.21–25); wording with respect.
- **"36 years":** 16.1.1, 16.2.2, 16.3.18.

## Sensitivity (the most sensitive episode of the season)
- **Krishna is worshipped.** He's never shown as guilty, beaten or helpless. He accepts, knows and allows; the end is a return in light, not a corpse. Arrow and wound only suggested.
- **Gandhari is a grieving mother,** truthful and devoted to dharma (the text's own words: *satyavādinī, dharmajñā*). Not bitter, never a villain.
- **Women's grief and the battlefield:** lamps, broken chariots, a horizon; no bodies.
- The drinking at Prabhasa: don't make it the joke or the moral.
- The hunter didn't know; he's forgiven.
