**Headline:** the whole story is in the **critical edition**, and Ganguli matches it closely. The research turned up a thread the whole episode can hang on: **one charge, "you could have stopped it, and you looked on", used three times.**

## The thread: "able, and looked on"
| When | Who says it | Words | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before the war**, on his way to ask for peace | **Krishna**, to Vidura | Let no one say: *"Krishna was **able**, yet did not hold back the raging Kurus and Pandavas."* I've come to try, for both sides. | CE 5.91.16–17 |
| **After the war** | **Gandhari**, to Krishna | *You were **able**. Why did you **look on** while they were destroyed?* (*upekṣitā… śaktena*) | CE 11.25.36–38 |
| **After the Yadavas die** | **Vyasa**, to Arjuna | Krishna **looked on**, though **able** to turn it away. (*upekṣitaṃ… śaktenāpi*) | CE 16.9.26; Mausala §8 |

So the charge he set out to prevent is the charge she made, and her curse comes true by his doing exactly that. **Say it as Vyasa does, as knowledge and acceptance, not failure:** "he could have changed the three worlds; what is a curse?" (16.9.27).

**Fairness:** Krishna did try to stop the war. He went as envoy (5.91, 5.93), and Duryodhana refused. Gandhari herself had pleaded with Duryodhana in that court (5.127.19–53). The text says that in her grief she **laid the blame on him** (11.25.35).

## Traps in the text (read before scripting)
1. **She doesn't curse the Pandavas, but she nearly does.** "She forgave them" fits 11.15.8 (*vigatakrodhā*, anger gone; comforted them like a mother). "She spared them" is safest.
2. **The epic's main cause for the Yadavas' end is the sages' curse** (the pestle). 16.5.3, 16.8.30, 16.9.8 and 16.9.25 all say *brahmaśāpa*. Gandhari's is named twice, both times **in Krishna's own mind** (16.3.19, 16.5.17). Don't say hers is the *only* cause. Best: "her words came true".
3. **Vyasa's line (16.9.26–27) is about the sages' curse.** The wording is the same as Gandhari's; the curse he means isn't. Don't put her name in his mouth.
4. **He isn't only a bystander at Prabhasa** (rows 26–28). Don't say "he did nothing". A true short line: "he could have stopped it. He let it come." or "and this time too, he let it happen".
5. **Not "God was punished".** He knows, he accepts, he acts to make it true. Never "defeated", "killed by a curse", "guilty".
6. **The smile isn't mockery:** *īṣad abhyutsmayann iva*, "as if slightly smiling".
7. **His stern words to her** (row 20) are part of the scene. Leaving them out of a short telling is fine; **don't say he comforted her there** (he had consoled her earlier, 9.62.55–65).
8. **"In thirty-six years"**: text, "when the thirty-sixth year comes". Ganguli: "the thirty-sixth year from this".
9. **The blindfold:** she stays blindfolded. She sees the field by divine sight; she looks at Yudhishthira's toes through the cloth's edge. She doesn't take it off.
10. **The arrow:** the sole (CE *pādatale*); heel in Ganguli. He wasn't hit in the toe or a jewel.
11. **Jara:** in 16.2.10 *jarā* (it can also mean "old age"); in 16.5.19 a hunter's name.
12. **"He died"**: the text says he left his body and went to his place; Arjuna finds the bodies of Krishna and Balarama and cremates them (16.8.31). "Left the world" is respectful and true.
13. **Nothing in these chapters says she regretted the curse,** or that he thanked her. (Ganguli's Krishna says the curse *helped* him: "thou hast aided me in the accomplishment of that task"; the CE reading is different and obscure, 11.25.44.) So "he thanked her" is out.
14. **"Blind Gandhari":** she's blindfolded by choice (though 11.14.21 does call the two "the blind couple").

## Versions
- **Scripture, critical edition:** the table above.
- **Ganguli (vulgate) adds or changes:** the curse "aided me"; heel for sole; one sore nail; Krishna "wished to die… for upholding the three worlds" and to make the words of "Atri's son" (Durvasa) true (§4).
- **Puranas (separate tellings):**
  - **[Vishnu Purana 5.37](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/vishnu-purana-wilson/d/doc116059.html):** only the sages' curse; **no Gandhari**. A piece of the pestle, swallowed by a fish, becomes the hunter's arrowhead. Krishna sends the hunter to heaven. Durvasa's words behind the foot.
  - **[Bhagavata Purana 11.30](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-bhagavata-purana/d/doc1129002.html):** no Gandhari in the verses read (to 35); a pippala tree; his foot looks like a deer's face; the arrow tipped with the pestle's last piece.
  - **The fish and the arrowhead aren't in the epic** (Wilson's own note 5 says so of the unbroken piece). Keep them out.
- **Retellings, not found in the passages read:**
  - **Jara is Vali reborn** (Rama's arrow repaid): not in CE 16.2–5 or the Puranas read. A lexicon cites Mausala 7.31; CE 16.8.31 is only the cremation. **UNSOURCED.**
  - **Gandhari takes off the blindfold to make Duryodhana's body like iron, and Krishna tricks him:** **UNSOURCED** in what was read. What the epic says is different: Danavas tell Duryodhana that Shiva made his upper body of diamond-hard stuff, and the Goddess his lower body of flowers (CE 3.240.5–8).
  - Other stories about Gandhari's past lives or the battlefield night that commenters may bring up were **not looked up**; treat any of them as **UNSOURCED**.
- **Place names today:** Bhalka Tirth near Somnath is the traditional site of the arrow. A pilgrimage tradition, not in the epic passage.

## Hook candidates
1. **"A mother cursed Krishna. He smiled, and made it come true."** (11.25.39–43; 16.3.21) True in both halves. **Recommended.**
2. **"She spared the men who killed her hundred sons. She cursed Krishna."** (11.15.8; 11.25)
3. **"Krishna knew the year his clan would die. A mother had told him."** (16.3.18–19)
4. **"Why didn't Krishna stop the war? One mother asked him."** (11.25.36) The question every viewer has. **Strongest for comments.**
5. *Rejected:* "The last curse of the Mahabharata fell on Krishna." Not true: the sages' curse on Samba comes later (16.2).

## Closing the loop
- **In the story:** *You were able, and you looked on* → thirty-six years later, able, he lets it come. End on the hunter: the one who wounded him is comforted.
- **With E02:** a man shot in mistake for a deer. There the archer was cursed; here he's comforted. **Season rhyme:** a season of curses ends with someone who could curse, and doesn't. (The text gives no curse from Krishna here; "could have cursed" is the narrator's line, so phrase it simply: *"He didn't curse him. He comforted him."* Both are true to 16.5.21.)
- **With E07:** foretold deaths: Parikshit built walls, Krishna walked toward his. **Order in time:** Parikshit's is later than Krishna's. Don't say otherwise.

## Clarity notes for the script (E06 lesson)
- **Must be said:** who she is (queen who bound her own eyes, mother of the Kauravas), that all her sons are dead, **why she blames Krishna** (he could have stopped it), what the curse says (same end for his clan, thirty-sixth year, alone in a forest), that he accepts, how it comes true.
- **Say the peace mission?** One clause would be fair ("He had asked for peace, and been refused"). Without it Krishna looks guilty. **Recommended to keep.**
- **Cut if short:** Vyasa, the toenails (beautiful, but its own scene), Samba and the pestle, Satyaki and Kritavarma, Balarama, Durvasa, Dvaraka.
- **If the pestle is cut,** the grass that turns to iron needs no reason if it's only shown, not told.

## Sensitivity notes
- **Krishna:** calm and knowing throughout. No wound, no blood, no body; light, a feather, a flute or the discus at rest. "Left the world", not "was killed".
- **Gandhari:** dignified; a truthful woman in unbearable grief. Never "bitter" or "a curse-giver". Don't mock the blindfold.
- **The field:** lamps, broken wheels, women in silhouette, dusk. No bodies.
- **Prabhasa:** no comic drunkenness; silhouettes, grass, iron-coloured light.
- **The hunter:** an ordinary man, horrified, forgiven. Not a villain and not a caste marker (*lubdhaka*, hunter).
- **Many viewers hold the Bhagavata's telling:** name it with respect in the replies.
