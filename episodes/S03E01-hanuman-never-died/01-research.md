**Headline:** the whole story is in Valmiki's **critical edition**, and the Mahabharata repeats it independently. That makes it the best-sourced claim of the whole series. The research turned up a two-step answer to *why can't he die*:
1. **As a child, Indra gives him a boon: he will die only when he himself chooses** (4.65.26–27).
2. **As a grown hero, he makes the choice:** *let me live as long as Rama's story is told*, and Rama grants it (7.39.15–19). At the very end Rama holds him to it: *you chose to live; don't break your promise* (7.98.24–25).

So Hanuman isn't immortal by a curse or by nature. **He could die whenever he wished, and he asked not to, for as long as the story lasts.** The Mahabharata has Hanuman tell Bhima the same thing, ages later (3.147.37).

## The thread: "as long as the story is told"
| When | Who | Words | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **After Rama is crowned**, at the farewell to his allies | **Hanuman** to Rama | *My love for you is fixed forever, king. As long as I hear Rama's story on this earth, so long let life stay in my body.* | CE 7.39.15–16 (S 7.40) |
| Same moment | **Rama**, rising from his throne and embracing him | *So it will be, without doubt. As long as the worlds last, my story will last. As long as it is told, your life will stay in your body.* | CE 7.39.17–19 |
| **Rama leaving the world**; everyone wants to follow him | **Rama** to Hanuman | *You made up your mind to live; don't break your promise. As long as my stories are told in the world, keep your life, and keep your word.* | CE 7.98.24–25 (S 7.108) |
| **Ages later, in the Mahabharata** | **Hanuman** to Bhima | *When Rama was established on his throne, I asked him: may I live as long as Rama's story is in the worlds. He said, "So be it."* | CE 3.147.37; Ganguli §147 |

**The word to keep:** in 7.39.16 Hanuman says *śroṣye*, "**I hear**": *as long as I get to **hear** Rama's story*. In 3.147.39, apsaras and gandharvas sing Rama's deeds to him where he lives. That's a true, gentle ending: he stays to hear the story.

## Versions
- **Scripture (critical / core text):**
  - **Valmiki CE** has both steps: the death-at-will boon (4.65.27), and the asked-for boon to live as long as the story is told (7.39.16–19, confirmed at 7.98.24–25).
  - **The Mahabharata CE** independently has Hanuman alive in the Pandavas' time, citing the same boon (3.147.37).
  - **The Uttara Kanda's place:** most scholars see it as a later layer of the Ramayana. It is still Valmiki's text in every edition, and the Kishkindha boon (4.65) is in the older core. Say "the Ramayana says", no hedge needed.
- **Southern recension differences:**
  - Hanuman answers Rama at the departure ("I'll stay on earth as long as your story is told").
  - **Jambavan** is also told to live "till the Kali Yuga begins".
  - **Neither is in the CE.**
- **Later or devotional texts (say "in later tellings"):**
  - **Sita's blessing**, *ajar amar gunanidhi sut hohu* ("be ageless and deathless, my son"), is from **Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas** (Sundara Kanda, around doha 17; **to check**). It isn't in Valmiki, and many viewers know it. Don't mix it in as Valmiki's.
  - **The Hanuman Chalisa** is Tulsidas-tradition devotion, not a source for this.
- **The "seven chiranjeevi" verse** (*aśvatthāmā balir vyāso hanūmāṃś ca vibhīṣaṇaḥ / kṛpaḥ paraśurāmaś ca saptaite cirajīvinaḥ*):
  - **Origin not traced: UNSOURCED.** Popular sites credit the Padma Purana, others say no Puranic source can be found ([Jeevana Sutra](https://dixith.com/jeevana-sutra/en/ashvatthama-balir-vyaso-sapta-chiranjeevi-shloka-meaning/), [Dharmayana](https://dharmayana.in/articles/who-are-the-chiranjivis-in-hindu-tradition)). Neither is a scholarly source.
  - Call it "**a well-known traditional verse**", never "the Ramayana / Mahabharata says".
- **TV / pop culture (never present these as scripture):**
  - "**Hanuman is alive today**" sightings, the Matang tribe's "Setu Asia" chant, "lives in the Himalayas".
  - "**Hanuman flew to swallow the sun**" (the text: he leaped to grab it as a fruit; Indra struck him; *swallowed* is pop).
  - "**Immortal**": the texts say he lives on **as long as the story is told / the worlds last**. Valmiki doesn't use the word *amara* here; Brahma calls him *dīrghāyuḥ*, "long-lived" (7.36.19). Use "never died / still living", not "can never die".

## Hook candidates
1. **Hanuman could die whenever he wanted. That was a god's boon. So why is he still alive?** (4.65.27; 7.39.16)
2. **Rama offered to take everyone with him when he left the world. He told one friend to stay.** (7.98.24–25)
3. **Hanuman didn't ask for power. He asked to hear one story, forever.** (7.39.16, *śroṣye*)
4. **Thousands of years after the Ramayana, Bhima met an old monkey he couldn't move. It was Hanuman, and he told him why he was still alive.** (3.147.37)
5. **Hanuman will live as long as one thing lasts: Rama's story.** And by watching this, you're keeping it alive. (7.39.18–19)

**Suggested spine (one plain thread):** hook (1) → the baby, the sun, the thunderbolt, the name, Indra's boon (die only by choice) → after the war, Rama asks his friends to go home; Hanuman asks to live as long as he hears the story → the embrace and the necklace → Rama leaves the world, everyone follows, he holds Hanuman to his word → payoff: so wherever the story is told, he's said to be listening ("…like now"). The Bhima meeting (rows 18–20) can be a single line ("even in the Mahabharata, ages later, Bhima met him") or kept for another video.
