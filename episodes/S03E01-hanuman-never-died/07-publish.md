**Accuracy:**
- **Both steps are in Valmiki's critical edition:**
  - Indra's boon, "your death only when you wish it" (CE 4.65.26–27).
  - Hanuman's own request to live as long as Rama's story is told, granted by Rama (7.39.14–19) and confirmed when Rama leaves (7.98.24–25).
- **The Mahabharata repeats it independently:** Hanuman tells Bhima (CE 3.147.37).
- **"Grab" the sun, never "swallow" it.** He leaped to grab it, thinking it a fruit (4.65.19–20).
- **"Never died" / "still living", not "can never die" or "immortal".** The texts say *as long as the story is told / the worlds last*. Valmiki's Brahma calls him "long-lived" (7.36.19).
- **"It's said he's listening" is tradition.** It rests on his own word, *to hear* the story (7.39.16), and on gandharvas singing it to him (MBh 3.147.39).
- **The seven-Chiranjeevi verse is a traditional verse. Its source isn't traced**, so never say "the Ramayana says" about the list.
- **Keep out of the copy:**
  - Sita's *ajar amar* blessing, which is Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas, not Valmiki.
  - Present-day sightings.

## Comment replies to have ready
- *"Hanuman is alive today, people have seen him!"* → The Ramayana's promise is that he lives **as long as Rama's story is told**. Sightings are faith and folk belief, not something the texts record. 🙏
- *"Sita gave him the boon: ajar amar gunanidhi sut hohu."* → That beautiful line is from Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas (Sundarkand). We told Valmiki's version, where Hanuman asks Rama himself (Uttara Kanda 40).
- *"He swallowed the sun!"* → In Valmiki he leaps to **grab** it, thinking it a fruit, and Indra's thunderbolt stops him (Kishkindha 66, Uttara 35). "Swallowed" comes from later retellings.
- *"Didn't Bhima meet Hanuman in the Mahabharata?"* → Yes! An old monkey lay across Bhima's path and asked him to move his tail. Bhima couldn't lift it. It was Hanuman, who told him this same boon from Rama (Vana Parva §146–147).
- *"Who are the seven Chiranjeevi?"* → A well-known traditional verse names Ashwatthama, Bali, Vyasa, Hanuman, Vibhishana, Kripa and Parashurama, and Markandeya is often added. We're telling each one's reason, one by one. Next: Bali.
- *"Why did Hanuman forget his powers?"* → As a child he troubled sages at their hermitages, and they cursed him to forget his strength until reminded (Uttara Kanda 36). That's why Jambavan reminds him before the leap to Lanka.
- *"Chiranjeevi means immortal?"* → It means long-lived. In Valmiki, Brahma calls him *dirghayu* ("long-lived"), and Rama's words tie his life to the story's: "as long as it's told".
- *"Why didn't Rama take Hanuman with him?"* → Hanuman had chosen to stay, and Rama held him to it: "You chose to live; don't break your promise" (Uttara Kanda 108). He also asked Vibhishana to stay and rule Lanka. That story is coming in this series.
- *"Where does Hanuman live?"* → In the Mahabharata, Bhima finds him on the Gandhamadana mountain, in a banana grove, where gandharvas and apsaras sing Rama's story to him (Vana Parva §146–147).
- *"Jambavan is a Chiranjeevi too!"* → In some versions of the Ramayana Rama tells Jambavan to live until the Kali age (Uttara Kanda 108, southern text). He isn't on the traditional list of seven.
