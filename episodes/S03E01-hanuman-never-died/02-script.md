# Script: S03E01, The One Who Asked to Live as Long as a Story

**Spine:** a god gives Hanuman death only by his own choice → (how: the sun, the thunderbolt, the jaw, the name, the boon) → he grows up and helps Rama → asked what he wants, he asks to live as long as Rama's story is told → Rama grants it → when Rama leaves the world, he holds Hanuman to it → so he stays, and is said to listen wherever the story is told.
**The hook's promise and its payoff:** beat 1 says *he could choose when to die, and never did*. Beats 5 (the choice), 8–9 (why he never chose), 11 (Rama holds him to it) and 12 (so he's still here) pay it off in order.
**Opening:** a new series sting: **चिरंजीवी I / CHIRANJEEVI I** (≈1 s). It needs a design; see Storyboard notes.
**Length:** EN 226 words ≈ 88 s, HI 232 words ≈ 91 s, both with the sting, at E07/E08's measured rates (2.6 / 2.58 words/s). A first draft at 296 words (≈115 s) was cut to one thread: Bhima's meeting and the necklace line went to the replies.
**Source used:** Valmiki critical edition, 4.65, 7.35–36, 7.39, 7.98; Mahabharata CE 3.147 (background only). **The research rows are not yet verified by the owner.**
**Source on screen:** "Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha & Uttara Kanda" / "वाल्मीकि रामायण, किष्किंधा और उत्तर कांड".

## Accuracy notes
- **Beat 1:** "a god" = Indra (4.65.26–27). Brahma also gave "no weapon can kill him" (4.65.25); only Indra's is the one about choosing.
- **Beat 2:** 4.65.19–20 ("thinking it a fruit, you leaped into the sky"). **"Grab", not "swallow"** (pop). Son of Vayu: 4.65.27–28.
- **Beat 3:** 4.65.21–22; 7.35.46–47 (left jaw, on a mountain peak); the name, 4.65.22 and 7.36.11. In the Uttara he goes after Rahu and Airavata too: left out.
- **Beat 4:** 4.65.23 (in the three worlds the wind didn't blow); 7.35.48–49 (he blocked the breath of all beings). "All the air in the world" is a plain rendering.
- **Beat 5:** 4.65.24–27; 7.36.9–24. **The Uttara list of boons doesn't repeat Indra's "death by choice"**: that line is Kishkindha 4.65.26–27, said by Jambavan as he tells the story. Plain "the gods gave gifts" covers both.
- **Beat 6:** the epic as a whole; in short, MBh 3.147.34–35 (he crossed the ocean and found Sita). The war and Ravana are left out. "Most devoted friend": *bhakti* and *sneha*, his own words at 7.39.15.
- **Beat 7:** 7.38–39: the farewell to his allies after the coronation. "Just one thing": the narrator's; he asks one thing.
- **Beat 8:** 7.39.16: *yāvad rāmakathāṃ vīra śroṣye 'haṃ pṛthivītale, tāvac charīre vatsyantu mama prāṇāḥ*, "as long as I hear Rama's story on earth, so long may life stay in my body". "Let me live, and hear it" keeps *śroṣye*; "is told" comes from Rama's reply (7.39.19).
- **Beat 9:** 7.39.17–19: rose from the seat, embraced him; "as long as the worlds last, my story will last; as long as it's told, your life will stay". "And so will you" compresses this. The necklace (7.39.20–21) is in the picture only.
- **Beat 10:** 7.98.17–20.
- **Beat 10:** "left the world": the *mahaprasthana*, he returns to his divine form (7.99–100). Not "died".
- **Beat 11:** 7.98.24–25: "you set your mind on living; don't break your promise; as long as my stories are told in the world, hold on to life and keep your word". "Stay" compresses the last part. Vibhishana is also told to stay (7.98.22): left for S03E03.
- **Beat 12:** "Chiranjeevi" from the traditional verse (origin **unsourced**: not cited on screen). **"It's said… he's listening"** is framed as tradition. It rests on his own wish to *hear* (7.39.16) and on gandharvas singing Rama's story to him (MBh 3.147.39), not on a verse saying "wherever". Keep "it's said" / "कहते हैं". The Mahabharata meeting with Bhima (3.147.37) backs "he stayed": in the replies.
- **Beat 13:** Bali and Vamana (Bhagavata 8.18–23; to research). "Dwarf": Vamana appears as a dwarf brahmin boy. **Confirm the next episode is Bali before voicing.**

## Sensitivity
- **Hanuman is a deity.** He's shown as strong, gentle and devoted: no cartoon monkey, no clowning. Even as a baby he's dignified (curious, bright), and the fall is a flash, not an injury close-up.
- **Rama** is calm and royal; the departure is light, not death.
- **Indra** isn't a villain: the text says he wasn't very angry (*nātikruddhaḥ*, 7.35.46) and he gives the greatest boon.
- No "monkey" jokes in the narration or captions.
