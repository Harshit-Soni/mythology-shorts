# Script: S01E05, The Warrior Cursed Twice

**Spine:** why did Karna's wheel sink? *He wants one weapon → he lies to get it → the lie's price is two curses → both come due in one moment.* Each beat follows from the last (S01E04 lesson). Beats 1 and 2 are the hook and the "trailer" (24 Nov research); beat 13 closes both.
**Opening:** the series sting (**शाप V / CURSED V**, ≈1 s).
**Length:** EN 196 words ≈ 80 s, HI 214 words ≈ 84 s, both including the 1 s sting (at E04 v2's measured rates). Over the skill's 60 s budget on purpose: the owner prefers a flowing ~60–75 s telling, and accepts small overruns.
**Source used:** Narada's account (Shanti Parva, CE 12.2–3) for the curses, and CE 8.66–67 for the battle. **The research rows are not yet verified by the owner.**
**Source on screen:** "Mahabharata, Shanti & Karna Parva" / "महाभारत, शान्ति और कर्ण पर्व".

## Accuracy notes (each shortened line against its row)
- **Beat 1:** "Few ask why" is the narrator's framing. In the epic, Yudhishthira himself asks it (row 0).
- **Beat 3:** CE: to be Arjuna's equal in battle. Drona's rule (a vow-keeping Brahmin, **or** an ascetic Kshatriya; row 2) is left out for time rather than shortened to "Brahmins only", which would be wrong. His partiality for Arjuna and his distrust of Karna are also left out.
- **Beat 4:** he says "a Brahmin **of Bhrigu's line**"; shortened.
- **Beat 5:** unknowing, and he confesses himself (row 4). Narada doesn't name the weapon used; Karna's own account says an arrow (row 14, trap 3). The arrow is only in the picture.
- **Beat 6:** "your rival" = "him you always strive against". "Careless" = *pramatta*, used in the curse of **both** Karna at the cow's death and Karna at his own. His enemy cutting off his head is left out.
- **Beat 7:** Narada tells the teaching after the cow, so the order holds (row 7). "It" is the Brahmastra.
- **Beat 8:** "An insect": no claim on whether it was Alarka or Indra (trap 4).
- **Beat 9:** the quotation shortens CE 12.3.25 ("No Brahmin could bear this great pain… tell me the truth"). He also calls it a Kshatriya's endurance (cut).
- **Beat 10:** "charioteer's son" / "सूतपुत्र" for his own word *Suta*. The curse (12.3.30–31): the weapon will serve him **except** at the time of his death, against an equal. "At the hour of your death, it will fail you" keeps that limit; "against your equal" is cut.
- **Beat 11:** CE 8.66.42 names both curses in one verse. "Would not come" / "प्रकट न हुआ" = *apratibhāti*. **Never "he forgot it"**: he invokes a Brahmastra again a few verses later (trap 1). **Likely viewer question; have the reply ready.**
- **Beat 12:** *cakre viṣaktam*, caught up with the wheel (8.67.23). Not "unarmed", not "in the back" (trap 7). **Left out:** "Wait a moment", and Krishna's answer about Draupadi (rows 17–18), so this telling is gentler on Karna than the text. The pinned comment should carry Krishna's answer.
- **Beat 13:** the wheel ← the cow's curse; the weapon ← the lie, which the insect exposed.
- **Beat 14:** checked 2026-11-24 in the critical edition: Nahusha, then lord of the gods, touches Agastya's head with his foot and is cursed to roam the earth as a great snake for ten thousand years ([CE 5.17.11–16](https://bombay.indology.info/mahabharata/text/UR/MBh05.txt)).

## Sensitivity
- Karna's birth is stated once, in his own words, with no moral drawn. Drona's rule isn't in the script.
- The cow's death, the insect's bite and Karna's death stay symbolic: a bell on the sand, a thin red line, a setting sun.

**Series tone:** Karna is neither villain nor victim: he lied knowingly, he killed the cow unknowingly, and he pays for both.
