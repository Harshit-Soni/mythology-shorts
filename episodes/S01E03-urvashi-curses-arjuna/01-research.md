# Research: S01E03

**Main source:** the Mahabharata in K. M. Ganguli's public-domain translation (the older *vulgate* text), read on sacred-texts.com on 2026-11-17:
- the curse: **Vana Parva §44–46** (the Indralokagamana sub-parva)
- the payoff: **Virata Parva §2, §11, §36–45, §65–66, §72**
- the family link: **Adi Parva §75**

> ⚠️ **The biggest accuracy point for this episode: the Urvashi curse is very likely NOT in the BORI critical edition.**
> - **The critical edition has these scenes:** Arjuna in Indra's heaven (Urvashi among the apsaras who dance for him), Indra handing him to Chitrasena to learn the gods' music and dance ("a skill that will serve him well"), and later the Brihannala disguise.
> - **It has no scene where Urvashi comes to Arjuna or curses him.** The Vyasa project's chapter-by-chapter retelling of the critical Aranyaka Parva has Chitrasena's scenes but no Urvashi curse, and no Urvashi page at all. Wikipedia's *Chitrasena* article calls it "a myth, rejected in the Critical Edition" (citing Mani's *Puranic Encyclopedia* and Leslie).
> - **Not yet confirmed:** the exact appendix number in the critical edition (*crit. ed. TBC*). The critical text itself hasn't been checked directly.
> - **Even Ganguli's Virata Parva never mentions the curse.** As Brihannala, Arjuna tells Prince Uttara he took up this "**vow**" in obedience to another's will (§45).
>
> **What this means for the script (CLAUDE.md rule 1):**
> - Tell the curse as the traditional telling, with a phrase like "in the Mahabharata's longer telling…" / "महाभारत की प्रचलित कथा में…".
> - Don't present it as the core text.
> - Everything from Arjuna's dance lessons to Brihannala's victory **is** in the core text and can be stated plainly.
> - The pinned comment and the replies should say this openly. It's a strong, honest talking point.

## Versions
- **Critical edition (core text):**
  - In the core text:
    - Arjuna in Amaravati
    - his weapons
    - Indra sending him to Chitrasena to learn the gods' dance and music, a skill that "will serve him well"
    - Urvashi dancing at the assembly
    - the Brihannala disguise, his life as a dance teacher, the cattle raid and the Uttara/Abhimanyu coda
  - **Not in the core text (very likely):** Urvashi's visit and the curse (rows 4–9). See the warning at the top. *Crit. ed. appendix number TBC.*
- **Vulgate (Ganguli and most Hindi tellings):** the full Urvashi episode, rows 4–9, with Indra turning the curse into a one-year blessing for the thirteenth year. This is the version everyone knows. It's an old and important part of the tradition, so present it as the traditional telling, not as invented.
- **Later and regional retellings:**
  - The Kathakali play *Urvashi Shapam* / *Shapamochanam* stages this story (Kerala) ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/social-sciences-and-humanities/2b3c749e-ac60-58fd-aa09-00267a2d7dfd/sapamochanam-the-curse-liberation-a-kathakali-play-by-sadanam-harikumar)).
  - Some retellings have **Urvashi herself** reduce the curse to a year, at Indra's request ([Anaadi](https://www.anaadi.org/post/mahabharata-vana-parva-part-2)). In Ganguli, **Indra** gives it the time limit; Urvashi isn't involved again.
- **Pop-culture and web versions (never present these as scripture):**
  - "**Urvashi was Arjuna's dance teacher**" and "he refused because she was his teacher" ([Wikipedia: Brihannala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brihannala)): **wrong**. Chitrasena taught him, and Arjuna's reason is that she is the **mother of his line**.
  - "**She wanted to marry him**": the text doesn't say marriage.
  - "**Arjuna turned into a woman**": the text says *klība* (neither man nor woman), **dressed** as a woman, "without manhood".
  - "**Arjuna chose which year**": in Ganguli, **Indra** says it will be the thirteenth year.
  - "**The curse made the disguise possible**": that's the vulgate. In the core Virata Parva, Arjuna calls it a **vow**.

## Hook candidates
1. **"Arjuna said no to the most beautiful woman in heaven, and was cursed for it."** (Vana §46, vulgate)
2. **"A curse became the perfect disguise."** The punishment hid him exactly when the Pandavas had to vanish. (Vana §46 → Virata §2)
3. **"Why did Arjuna call a goddess 'mother'?"** Urvashi was the wife of Pururavas, the first ancestor of his line. (Adi §75, Vana §46)
4. **"The dance teacher who beat Bhishma, Drona and Karna in one day."** (Virata §65)
5. **"Indra knew. He made his son learn to dance years before he'd need it."** (Vana §44, which **is** in the critical edition, so this hook is safe)

## Sensitivity notes (for script and storyboard)
- **Urvashi:** the vulgate describes her at length and sensually. **Don't.** Show her with dignity, as a celestial dancer in Indra's court and a proud, wounded figure when she curses (CLAUDE.md rule 2: no sexualisation). Show the night visit as Arjuna bowing with his eyes closed and his hands over his ears, not her appearance.
- **Brihannala and the third gender:** "scorned as a eunuch" is the **curse's** wording. The video must not mock Brihannala or treat being *klība* as a joke. Show Brihannala as dignified and gifted (the best dance teacher in the palace, the hero who drives the chariot). The twist is about **disguise and timing**, not humiliation.
- **Urvashi isn't a villain:** in the text she came at Indra's command and argues from the apsaras' own customs. Show both sides fairly. That's also a good comment-section question.
