# Research: S01E01, The Sage Who Put Dharma on Trial

> ⚠️ **Correction to the original idea:** In the Mahabharata the god Mandavya curses is **Dharma**, the god of justice, not Yama. Later texts and many retellings identify Dharma with Yama (Yama is also called *Dharmaraja*), which is why "Mandavya cursed Yama" is a common version. The script should say **Dharma, the god of justice**. It can add "whom many know as Yama" only as a secondary note, never as what the epic says.

**Source chapter numbers:** Ganguli's translation (older *vulgate* text) has this in Adi Parva §106–108. The BORI critical edition has it in **Adi Parva 1.100–101**.

## Versions
- **Scripture (Mahabharata core text):** Everything in the table above. The god is **Dharma**. The insect was pierced with **a blade of grass**. The age limit in the critical edition is **14**. A piece of the stake **stays inside him**, which is where the name Ani-Mandavya comes from.
- **Ganguli / vulgate differences:** the age is given as both "twelfth year" and "fourteen" (an inconsistency in the text he translated). In Ganguli the rishis come as unspecified "birds"; in the critical-edition translation they are vultures.
- **Puranas:** The *Brahmanda Purana* adds a separate story in which Mandavya, while on the stake, curses Ugrashravas (husband of the devoted wife Shilavati) to die before sunrise. Shilavati stops the sun from rising until Anasuya intervenes. Some versions say **Shiva** gave Mandavya his long life. **Not in the epic, so leave it out of this episode** (it could be a future episode). Source: [Wikipedia: Mandavya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandavya), citing Nanditha Krishna, *Sacred Animals of India*.
- **Popular retellings (not scripture):**
  - "Mandavya cursed **Yama**": a common identification of Dharma with Yama (Dharmaraja). Fine as an aside, but not what the epic says.
  - The childhood act grows into "tortured birds and bees" or "ants and bugs". **The epic says one insect, with a blade of grass.**
  - The age limit varies: **5, 12 or 14** across retellings (Wikipedia cites a "before age five" version from George Williams' *Handbook of Hindu Mythology*). **Use 14** (critical edition).
  - Some retellings say a **trident**. The epic's word is *shula*, a stake or spike. Show a **stake**.

## Hook candidates
1. **"The god who judges every soul was once put on trial, by a man he'd wronged."** (the reversal; strongest)
2. "As a child, he hurt an insect with a blade of grass. Years later, he was impaled for it." (justice and horror; the epic gives no age, so don't say one)
3. "This is why, in Hindu tradition, a child under 14 can't commit a sin." (an origin story people can share; the "in Hindu tradition" framing must stay "according to the Mahabharata")
4. "Vidura was the wisest man in the Mahabharata. He was also a god serving a sentence." (fans of Vidura)
5. "He sat silent for years. That silence got him executed." (mystery)
