## Comment replies to have ready
- *"It was Yama, not Dharma!"* → The Mahabharata names **Dharma**, the god of justice (Adi Parva §108). Yama is often called Dharmaraja, so later retellings merge the two, but the epic's word is Dharma.
- *"The age was 12 / 5, not 14"* → Ganguli's translation has both "twelfth year" and "fourteen" in the same speech; the BORI critical edition reads **14**. Some later books give 5.
- *"Shiva saved him on the stake" / the Shilavati story* → Those come from the Puranas (e.g. Brahmanda Purana), not the Mahabharata. Could be a future episode.
- *"A servant woman? The text says Shudra."* → Yes: the epic says Dharma would be born of a Shudra woman. Vidura's mother was Ambika's maid, freed by Vyasa (Adi Parva §106).
- *"Isn't punishing a child's mistake unfair?"* → That's exactly Mandavya's argument, and why he set the new law. Thank them and ask which side they're on.
