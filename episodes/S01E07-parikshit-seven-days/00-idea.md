# S01E07: The King With Seven Days to Live

- **Series:** S01 Shraap: Curses That Shaped the Mahabharata (episode 7)
- **Idea (one line):** A tired king, ignored by a silent sage, hangs a dead snake on the sage's shoulder. The sage's young son curses him to die by Takshaka's bite within seven days. The king walls himself up on a tower held by a single pillar. It isn't the guards who fail: the one man who could save him is paid to turn back, and the king himself, laughing, puts the last snake to his own neck.
- **Why it will work:**
  - It pays off E06's tease ("a dead snake, and a king with seven days to live").
  - A countdown: every viewer feels seven days.
  - The end is a shock few know: the snake came hidden as a tiny worm in a fruit, and **he invited it as a joke**.
  - Parikshit is Arjuna's grandson, the child who carries on the Pandavas' line. The war's survivor dies over one rude moment.
- **Created:** 2026-11-25

## Angle (from the critical edition, 1.36–40, read 2026-11-25; research still to be done properly)
- **One spine:** one rash moment → one rash curse → seven days shutting every way in → **two ways left open: a bribe, and his own laugh.** Each beat leads to the next.
- **Hook options:**
  1. *A king knew the day he would die. He shut out every snake. He let the last one in himself.*
  2. *A dead snake killed a king.*
- **Plant and pay off:**
  - The way he dies sits inside the curse ("Takshaka, within seven days").
  - "Make the sage's words true" is the king's own joke at sunset on the seventh day (1.39.30–31).
- **The turn:** Kashyapa, a healer who brings a burnt tree back to life in front of Takshaka, then goes home with Takshaka's gold.
- **The sage forgives; the boy doesn't.** Shamika tells his son the king was hungry, tired and didn't know of his vow, and sends a warning (1.37.20–27, 1.38.11–21). That fairness is the heart of it. The king grieves more for what he did than for dying (1.38.25).
- **Length:** ≤ 170 words in each language (75–80 s). E06 v2 grew to 84 / 91 s because reasons had to be put back in: choose fewer events, and give each a reason, from the first draft.
- **Clarity check before voicing (E06 lesson):** every event has a stated reason, every quote a named speaker, and the ending pays off something said earlier.

## Watch for in research
- **What's in the critical edition and what isn't:** Shuka and the seven-day Bhagavata, Kali in the king's crown, and the Ganga bank are from the **Bhagavata Purana** (to check). Keep them out of the narration, or flag them.
- **How he asked, and what state he was in:** tired, thirsty, hungry; a wounded deer lost; asked again and again (1.36.14–18, 1.37.6–8).
- **Where the snake was put and how:** with the tip of his bow, on the sage's shoulder (1.36.18–19). He regretted it at once (1.36.20).
- **Who taunted Shringi:** a friend, Krisha, jokingly (1.36.23–26). Is it needed?
- **"Within seven nights" or "on the seventh day":** 1.37.14, 1.37.19.
- **Takshaka's reasons:** here he only carries out the curse. The Khandava forest fire is another thread: don't join them unless the text does.
- **The end:** the worm, "copper-coloured, black-eyed" (1.39.29), placed on his neck. Only the coils, fire and the falling house are told after that (1.40.1–4).
- **Other tellings:** the ministers' account to Janamejaya later in the Adi Parva repeats the story with differences: compare.
- **Then E08:** Gandhari's curse on Krishna comes *earlier* in time (the end of the war). Decide how the tease bridges back; or tease the snake sacrifice.

## Sensitivity
- Shamika is gentle and fair; Shringi is a boy, hurt for his father. Nobody is mocked for being a Brahmin or a king.
- Parikshit isn't a villain: one wrong act, and real remorse.
- Death is symbolic: coils, a flare of light, an empty throne. No bite, no blood.
