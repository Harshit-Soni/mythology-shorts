**Headline:** the whole story is in the **critical edition**, and Ganguli matches it closely. But the version most viewers know, Shuka reciting the Bhagavata by the Ganga for seven days, is the **Bhagavata Purana's**, and it tells the seven days **in the opposite way**. Say "in the Mahabharata" once, early.

## Two very different stories of the seven days
| | **Mahabharata** (CE 1.36–40) | **Bhagavata Purana** (1.18–19, 12.6) |
|---|---|---|
| What he wants from the sage | news of a deer he shot (he's also tired, thirsty, hungry) | **water** |
| Why the sage is silent | a **vow of silence** | deep in meditation, eyes closed |
| The curse | Takshaka will kill you **within seven days** | a snake will bite you on the seventh day |
| What he does | a mansion **on one pillar**, guards, healers, mantras | gives up the kingdom, **fasts by the Ganga**, hears the Bhagavata from Shuka |
| How he feels | grieves more for what he did than for dying | welcomes it, fearless |
| How Takshaka comes | **as a worm in a fruit** the king puts to his own neck | disguised as a Brahmin; the king is already absorbed in Brahman |
| Kashyapa, paid to turn back | yes | yes (12.6.11–12) |

**The epic itself tells it twice.** The second time, the ministers tell his son Janamejaya (CE 1.45–46): no worm and no joke (Takshaka comes "in disguise" and burns him in the mansion), the king is **sixty and old** (1.45.23), and they explain who overheard Takshaka and Kashyapa: **a man gathering firewood in that tree, burnt with it and brought back to life with it** (1.46.29–31).

**Recommended:** follow the first epic account (1.36–40). Never mix in Shuka or the Ganga.

## Traps in the text (read before scripting)
1. **Not Shuka, not the Ganga, not fasting.** Those are the Bhagavata's. Expect "wrong!" comments, so say "in the Mahabharata".
2. **Not water.** He asks about his deer. His thirst is in the text (1.36.14); "he asked for water" is the Bhagavata's (1.18.27).
3. **On the shoulder, not "garlanded round the neck".** *Skandhe* (1.36.18).
4. **Within seven nights** (1.37.14), said again as "on the seventh day" (1.37.19).
5. **Shamika doesn't curse and doesn't approve.** The text takes his side.
6. **He isn't careless.** He guards himself well. What kills him is a bribe, a disguise, and one laugh. Don't say "a small mistake killed him" only.
7. **The bribe isn't pure greed:** Kashyapa also sees that the king's time is over (1.39.19). "Took the gold and turned back" is true; "betrayed him" goes further than the text.
8. **Kashyapa** here is a healer; don't call him Kashyapa the father of gods and snakes. Same or similar name only.
9. **Takshaka's motive** here is only the curse. The Khandava fire isn't mentioned in these chapters.
10. **"Let the words be true"** is half a joke, half atonement. Ganguli: "so that my sinful act may be expiated and the words of the ascetic rendered true". Ganguli says "smiled"; CE *prāhasat*, he laughed (or smiled broadly).
11. **Don't say the epic was told for the first time there.** It was *recited* there; Vyasa had taught it to his pupil before.
12. **He isn't "the first victim of Kali Yuga":** not in these chapters.
13. **His age** (sixty) is only in the second account.

## Versions
- **Scripture, critical edition:** the table above; both epic accounts.
- **Purana (Bhagavata):** Shuka and the seven days; water; Kali begging for a place to live and being given **gold** (1.17.38–39). The disguised Brahmin. In it, **Brihaspati** (not Astika) talks Janamejaya into stopping.
- **Retellings (not found in the passages read):** Kali sat in the king's **golden crown** and turned his mind. The gold is in 1.17.39; the crown is **UNSOURCED** here.
- **Wikipedia and blogs** give the fruit, the worm and the neck, usually as here.

## Hook candidates
1. **"A king knew the day he would die. He shut out every snake. Then he put one to his own neck."** (1.37.14, 1.38.28–30, 1.39.30–32) True, and a paradox. **Recommended.**
2. **"A dead snake killed a king."** (1.36.18 → 1.39.33) Shortest.
3. **"One man could have saved him. He was paid to go home."** (1.39.9–19)
4. **"The sage forgave the king. His son didn't."** (1.36.19, 1.37.20–27)
5. **"He died laughing."** (1.39.32–33)

## Closing the loop
- **In the story:** the first snake he lifted himself, with his bow; the last he lifted himself, with his fingers. *He put a dead snake on a sage, and a living one on himself.* Same gesture, both his own.
- **For the series:** the revenge for this curse is the snake sacrifice, where the Mahabharata was recited (row 24). **So one dead snake is why this whole story was told to a king.** A strong ending for a series about curses. Keep it to one line.
- **Tease E08:** Gandhari's curse on Krishna is earlier in time, at the war's end. Bridge through the telling, e.g. *"And in that story, one mother cursed Krishna himself."*
- **Link to E05:** again a *krimi*, a worm or insect, undoes someone.
- **Link to E02:** the text compares his hunting to Pandu's (1.36.9), whom a hunt also cursed.

## Clarity notes for the script (E06 lesson)
- **Cut if short of time:** Krisha's taunt, Gauramukha's name, the disguised snakes' kusha and water, Astika, Uttanka.
- **Reasons that must be said:** why the sage didn't answer (a vow); why the king snapped (tired, hungry, ignored); why the curse stood though the father objected (it can't be taken back); why the healer turned back (paid, and saw the king's time was over); why the king played with the worm (sunset; he thought he'd won).
- **Plant the sunset:** "within seven days" must be clear, so that the last sunset feels like victory.

## Sensitivity notes
- **Shamika** is the moral centre: gentle and fair. **Shringi** is a hurt boy, not a villain.
- **The cow-pen** where the sage sits drinking calves' froth (1.36.15): leave it out or keep it plain; don't make it comic.
- **Parikshit** is a good king. No sneer at Brahmins or at kings.
- **Death:** coils, light, an empty seat. No bite, no blood, no burning bodies.
- **The snake sacrifice** kills snakes: mention only in a clause, no spectacle.
- **The Bhagavata version is sacred to many:** name it with respect in the pinned comment.
