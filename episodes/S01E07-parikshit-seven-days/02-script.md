# Script: S01E07, The King With Seven Days to Live

**Spine:** *one rash act → one rash curse that can't be unsaid → every way in shut, but two: a bribe, and the king's own laugh.* He lifts a dead snake in anger and a living one in jest.
**Opening:** the series sting (**शाप VII / CURSED VII**, ≈1 s).
**Length:** EN 189 words ≈ 82 s, HI 197 words ≈ 84 s, both with the sting, at E06 v2's measured rates (2.34 / 2.36 words/s). A little over the 75 s aim; the ready cuts bring it to about 75 / 80 s.
**Source used:** the epic's first account (Adi Parva, CE 1.36–40). **The research rows are not yet verified by the owner.** The Bhagavata Purana's version (Shuka, the Ganga) is kept out and named in the pinned comment.
**Source on screen:** "Mahabharata, Adi Parva" / "महाभारत, आदि पर्व".

## Accuracy notes (each shortened line against its row)
- **Beat 1:** "knew when he'd die": within seven nights, by the curse and the warning (1.37.14, 1.38.19). "Shut out every snake" is the narrator's summary of 1.38.28–30.
- **Beat 2:** the deer he had wounded, lost in the forest (1.36.11–14). **"In the Mahabharata" is there on purpose** (the Bhagavata differs). "Arjuna's grandson" is in the name tag.
- **Beat 3:** he asks about the deer, **not for water**. "Hungry and tired": 1.36.14, 16; 1.37.7. The vow: 1.36.18.
- **Beat 4:** on the **shoulder**, with the bow's tip (1.36.18–19). His regret at once (1.36.20) is only in the picture.
- **Beat 5:** "forgave": 1.38.18 ("he forgave that act of yours; his son did not"). "In seven days" for "within seven nights" (1.37.14). The friend's taunt (1.36.23–26) is left out. The king isn't present at the curse, so the boy speaks of "the king".
- **Beat 6:** shortens 1.37.26 ("not knowing this vow, he did this"). "Can't be unsaid": the son says it can't be otherwise, and the father agrees (1.38.1–3).
- **Beat 7:** the warning: 1.38.11–21. One pillar, guarded, healers and mantras: 1.38.28–30 ("guards" stands for all of them).
- **Beat 8:** Kashyapa, 1.38.31–39.19. "Cure that poison" = cure the bite and revive what the venom burnt; the tree is in the picture. **He also saw that the king's time was over (1.39.19): not in the narration; the reply must have it.** "Paid" is exact.
- **Beat 9:** 1.39.22–29. Takshaka sent snakes disguised as ascetics with fruit; in the picture. The critical edition says he came out of that fruit (1.39.33); Ganguli, that he had entered it.
- **Beat 10:** 1.39.30–31: "The sun is setting; now I have no fear of poison. Let the sage's words be true; let this little worm, as Takshaka, bite me." "I'm safe" shortens the first half. Ganguli adds that he said it to atone.
- **Beat 11:** 1.39.32–33. The bite, the fire and the falling house (1.40.1–4) are only suggested.
- **Beat 12:** the narrator's reflection. "Lifted in jest": he laughed (*prāhasat*).
- **Left out on purpose:** the snake sacrifice, where the Mahabharata was recited (rows 22–24). It comes after the climax and starts a new story; it suits the pinned comment, or E08's finale. Say **"recited"**, never "first told".
- **Beat 13:** E08 (Gandhari, Stri Parva), not yet researched.

## Sensitivity
- Shamika is the moral centre, Shringi a hurt boy, Parikshit a good king with one wrong act.
- No bite, no blood, no burning bodies: coils of light, a red streak in the sky, an empty seat.
- The sage is shown simply seated; the cow-pen detail is left out.
