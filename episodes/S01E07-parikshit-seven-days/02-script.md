# Script: S01E07 v2, The King With Seven Days to Live

**v2 (2026-11-25):** a plain retelling. v1 is in `review/02-script-v1.md` (its voice, scenes and renders still exist). The owner's note on v1: super confusing, not clear.

**What was wrong, and what changed:**
- **The hook was a riddle** ("he put one to his own neck"). Now it says what the danger is.
- **The healer and the bribe were a side story** in the middle: a new man, a burnt tree, gold, gone. Cut. (They go in the replies.)
- **The king's joke made no sense cold** ("Let this worm be Takshaka, and the curse come true!"). Now we know the time is almost up, that he feels safe, and that it's a joke.
- **The curse was rushed into one line** with the forgiveness. Now: the sage stays calm; the son curses; the sage can't undo it, so he warns the king.
- **The last reflection was a puzzle.** Now it names both snakes and where he put them.

**Spine:** an angry mistake → a curse: seven days → he locks every snake out → on the last evening one comes in as a tiny worm → as a joke, he puts it on his own neck.
**Opening:** the series sting (**शाप VII / CURSED VII**, ≈1 s).
**Length:** EN 213 words ≈ 83 s, HI 219 words ≈ 86 s, both with the sting, at v1's measured rates (2.6 / 2.58 words/s).
**Source used:** the epic's first account (Adi Parva, CE 1.36–40). **The research rows are not yet verified by the owner.** The Bhagavata Purana's version is kept out and named in the pinned comment.
**Source on screen:** "Mahabharata, Adi Parva" / "महाभारत, आदि पर्व".

## Accuracy notes
- **Beat 1:** he is *told* by the sage's messenger (1.38.11–21). "Locked every snake out" sums up 1.38.28–30.
- **Beat 3:** "Have you seen my deer?" shortens his question (1.36.16–17). Not water (that's the Bhagavata's). **"In the Mahabharata" is now only on screen**; the pinned comment carries the other telling.
- **Beat 4:** on the shoulder, with the bow's tip (1.36.18–19). "Left": he went back to his city, troubled (1.36.20).
- **Beat 5:** "stayed calm": he said nothing, good or bad (1.36.19), and forgave it (1.38.18). "In seven days" for "within seven nights" (1.37.14). The friend's taunt is left out.
- **Beat 6:** 1.37.20–27; 1.38.1–3; the warning, 1.38.11–21.
- **Beat 7:** one pillar, guards, healers, mantras: 1.38.28–30.
- **Beat 8:** 1.39.20–29, 33. He sent snakes disguised as ascetics with the fruit (in the picture). The CE says he came out of that fruit; Ganguli, that he had entered it. **The healer Kashyapa (1.38.31–39.19) is left out, so nothing says the king had no other help: keep him for the replies.**
- **Beats 9–10:** 1.39.28–32: "The sun is setting; now I have no fear of poison… let this little worm, as Takshaka, bite me." "As a joke": he laughed (*prāhasat*). Ganguli adds that he said it to make up for his sin: for the replies.
- **Beat 11:** 1.39.33; 1.40.1–4. No bite shown or said.
- **Beat 12:** the narrator's.

## Sensitivity
- Shamika is the moral centre, Shringi a hurt boy, Parikshit a good king with one wrong act.
- No bite, no blood: coils of light, a red streak, an empty seat.
