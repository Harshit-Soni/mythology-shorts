## Comment replies to have ready
- *"Wrong! He sat by the Ganga and heard the Bhagavata from Shuka."* → That's the Bhagavata Purana's telling (1.19), and a beautiful one. This video follows the Mahabharata (Adi §40–44), where he shuts himself in a house on one pillar.
- *"He asked the sage for water."* → In the Bhagavata, yes (1.18). In the Mahabharata he's tired, hungry and thirsty, but he asks about the deer he had shot (Adi §40).
- *"He put the snake round the sage's neck."* → The text says shoulder, lifted with the tip of his bow (Adi §40).
- *"Kali was sitting in his golden crown!"* → We didn't find the crown in the texts we read. The Bhagavata does say Kali was given gold as one of his places (1.17).
- *"Wasn't there a healer who could save him?"* → Yes, the video leaves him out to keep it short. Kashyapa could cure Takshaka's bite; Takshaka paid him more than the king would, and he turned back. The text adds that he saw, by insight, that the king's time was over (Adi §42–43).
- *"So the healer was just greedy?"* → He did come for a reward, and Takshaka gave him more. But the text adds that he saw, by insight, that the king's time was over (Adi §43).
- *"Kashyapa is the father of the snakes!"* → The text just calls this one a learned Brahmin who can cure the bite. The Sanskrit name, Kashyapa with a long a, means "of Kashyapa's line".
- *"Why didn't the king just kill the worm?"* → The last sun was setting and he thought he was safe. He said: let the sage's words come true; let this be Takshaka (Adi §43). Ganguli's translation adds that he meant it to make up for his sin.
- *"Why not take the curse back?"* → Shringi says his words can't be false, even in jest, and his father agrees (Adi §42). So he sent a warning instead.
- *"The boy was wrong."* → His own father says so: the king was hungry, tired, and didn't know of the vow (Adi §41).
- *"Takshaka was taking revenge for the Khandava forest."* → These chapters don't mention it. Here he carries out the curse.
- *"What happened next?"* → His son Janamejaya began a snake sacrifice in revenge. A young Brahmin, Astika, had it stopped. And it was there that the Mahabharata was recited to the king. 🔥
- *"The text has another version."* → It does: later his ministers tell the story without the worm, and a wood-gatherer in the burnt tree is the witness (critical edition 1.45–46). We followed the first.
- *"Next: Gandhari?"* → Yes. Episode 8 is the mother who cursed Krishna. 🙏
