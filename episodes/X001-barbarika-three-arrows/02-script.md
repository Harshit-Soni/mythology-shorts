## Ending
The last beat pays off the hook: the warrior who could have won any war is worshipped as *the support of those who lose* (हारे का सहारा). It's a one-off, so there's no next-episode tease; the pinned comment invites the Skanda Purana version.

## Kept out of the video (description / pinned comment)
- **The Skanda Purana tells it differently:** the boast of "one muhurta", one ash arrow marking every hero's death-spot (even Krishna's sole), Krishna striking with his discus because of a past-life curse from Brahma, and the head's verdict of a Hari-Hara figure (SkP 66).
- Barbarika is not in the Vyasa Mahabharata or the critical edition.
- Folk variants: the tree is unnamed in the katha (Pattanaik says banyan); some say Shiva gave the arrows.

## Writing notes
- **Respect:** a living deity. The Hindi uses the respectful plural for Barbarika (उन्होंने, जाते, ले लीजिए); Krishna addresses him as तुम (as a god to a devotee). The beheading is never shown: an offering, a white flash, then the serene head in the sky.
- **Beat 9 logic (corrected 2026-11-10):** the side he *joins* starts **winning**, so the other side becomes the losing one and his vow forces him to switch, again and again, until both armies are destroyed. (The first version said the side he joined would lose, which was backwards.) This is the popular explanation of *why* Krishna asks (research → Versions: devotional retellings). It sits inside the "Khatu telling" frame set in beat 2.
- **Beat 12, "hilltop":** SkP says "the peak of the mountain"; the Khatu telling says the head watched the whole war.
- **Cue words in both languages:** beat 8 *Krishna / कृष्ण* (eye glow); beat 14 *Khatu / खाटू* (title).
