# Script: S01E06 v2, The King of Heaven Who Became a Snake

**v2 (2026-11-25), not yet voiced.** The owner asked whether v1 was clear. Read as a first-time viewer, its second half wasn't: the foot, the question and the end of the curse had no stated reason, and the quotes didn't say who was speaking. v1 is in `review/02-script-v1.md`; the v1 storyboard, voice, kit and videos are kept in `review/` and `render/v1/`.

**Spine:** a humble king → power changes him → a trap for his pride → he insults a sage → the curse, **with its way out** → the snake asks its question → the answer (*conduct*) is also why he fell → free.
**What each change fixes:**
- **Why he turned:** beat 4 says it ("power changed him"), so beat 12 has something to pay off.
- **Why it's a trap:** it's "for his pride".
- **Why the foot:** the sages rebuke him (5.17.10–11).
- **Why a riddle:** the curse itself says whoever answers his questions will free him (3.176.21). That replaces "Yudhishthira will free you", which named someone we hadn't met.
- **Who speaks:** every quote in beats 8–12 names its speaker, and the snake asks Yudhishthira directly (3.177.6–15).
- **Two endings made one:** beat 12 joins the answer ("conduct") to his own fall.
- **Cut:** the three-card trailer. It delayed the story, and its "riddle" meant nothing yet.

**Opening:** the series sting (**शाप VI / CURSED VI**, ≈1 s).
**Length:** EN 197 words ≈ 85 s, HI 212 words ≈ 92 s, both with the sting, at v1's measured rates (2.34 / 2.32 words/s). Longer than v1 (74 / 77 s), because the missing reasons are now said.
**Source used:** Udyoga Parva (CE 5.11–17) for the rise and the curse; Vana Parva (CE 3.175–178) for the way out and the release. **The research rows are not yet verified by the owner.**
**Source on screen:** "Mahabharata, Udyoga & Vana Parva" / "महाभारत, उद्योग और वन पर्व".

## Accuracy notes (each shortened line against its row)
Checked again in the critical edition on 2026-11-25 (5.11, 5.15, 5.17, 3.176–178).
- **Beat 1:** he is called a human king (*mānuṣo rājā*, 5.16.21). Beat 13 completes the story.
- **Beat 2:** why Indra vanished (guilt after Vritra) is left out. "I'm too weak": *durbalo 'ham* (5.11.3).
- **Beat 3:** 5.11.6, from the gods and sages.
- **Beat 4:** "Then power changed him": after the boon and the kingdom, "having been always righteous, he became a man of desire" (5.11.8); "on getting that lordship, pride came over me" (3.177.8). "Demanded": 5.11.14–15.
- **Beat 5:** it is **Indra's** plan, spoken by Shachi (5.15.1–13); the picture shows Indra behind it. "For his pride" is the narrator's: she praises him above gods and demons, and he is delighted (5.15.11–16). "No god ever has" shortens "not Vishnu's, nor Rudra's".
- **Beat 6:** "rebuked": the sages tell him he is set on adharma (5.17.10). The quarrel is over the Vedic hymns, which is left out. **"Set his foot on", never "kicked"** (5.17.11). "Rebuked" / "टोका" is said of the sages together; Agastya is one of them.
- **Beat 7:** **"ten thousand years" is left out** so it doesn't clash with beat 8 (research, trap 1).
- **Beat 8:** the snake's own account: falling, he asks for an end to the curse (3.176.18, 3.178.39), and the sage, moved by pity (3.176.19), says: *whoever, knowing the difference, answers the questions you ask will free you* (3.176.21). In 3.178.40 the sage also names Yudhishthira; not used. **The script moves from account A to B here**; both have Agastya cursing.
- **Beat 9:** "Ages later" / "बहुत समय बाद": no figure given. The snake's boon: 3.176.22.
- **Beat 10:** Yudhishthira finds Bhima in the coils (3.177.1); the snake tells him "I was the king named Nahusha, your forefather" (3.177.6) and "answer my questions and I'll release your brother" (3.177.12); the question: 3.177.15. It asks more than one thing; only the first is used.
- **Beat 11:** three of the seven qualities in 3.177.16. "Not birth alone" follows 3.177.20–21 and 25–28.
- **Beat 12:** "lost heaven by his own conduct" is the narrator's link, from his own words: drunk on lordship, he insulted the sages and was brought to this state (3.177.8–9; 3.178.31, 36–37). "Power deludes even the wise": 3.178.30 (*riddhi*, prosperity; then *aishvarya*, lordship: English "power", Hindi "वैभव").
- **Beat 13:** 3.178.32, 44–45. In the text the curse ends through talking with a good man, after more questions; the script's "let Bhima go, and the curse broke" keeps the result.
- **Beat 14:** a king (Parikshit) lays a dead snake on a sage's shoulder and is cursed to die within seven days ([CE 1.36.18, 1.37.11–19](https://bombay.indology.info/mahabharata/text/UR/MBh01.txt)).
- **Left out on purpose:** "Sarpa, sarpa!" (Devi Bhagavata) and Bhrigu (account C).

## Sensitivity
- Shachi is calm and clever; nothing suggestive. "Demanded" / "माँग बैठे" is as far as it goes.
- Beat 11 is Yudhishthira's own answer, close to the text, with no comment added.
- No bite, no blood: coils and light only.
