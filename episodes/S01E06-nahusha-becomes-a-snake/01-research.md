**Headline:** both halves of the story are in the **critical edition**, and they form one story: the fall (Udyoga) and the release (Vana). The epic tells the fall **three times**, with different details.

## The three accounts in the epic
| | **A. Agastya to Indra** (Udyoga, CE 5.17) | **B. The snake himself** (Vana, CE 3.176–178) | **C. Bhishma** (Anushasana, CE 13.102–103) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Why the sages carry him | Shachi's request, Indra's plan (5.15) | pride: "a thousand Brahmin sages carried my palanquin" | pride after a boon |
| What sets it off | he rejects the Vedic hymns; **touches Agastya's head with his foot** | his **foot touches Agastya**, who is carrying him | he goads Agastya, then **kicks his head with his left foot** |
| Who curses | **Agastya** | **Agastya** ("unseen") | **Bhrigu**, hidden in Agastya's hair |
| The curse | a great snake on earth for **ten thousand years**, then heaven again | "become a snake" | go to earth as a snake |
| The way out | the years run out | **Yudhishthira will free you** (3.178.40); whoever **answers your questions** (3.176.21) | **Yudhishthira** will free you (after Agastya pleads) |

**Recommended:** A for the rise, Shachi and the curse; B for the release. Both have Agastya. Use "touched Agastya's head with his foot" (A), and **leave out the ten thousand years**, so A and B don't clash. Say nothing that needs C.

## Traps in the text (read before scripting)
1. **How the curse ends differs.** Account A: ten thousand years. Account B: Yudhishthira, or whoever answers his questions. Don't combine them in one sentence.
2. **"Kicked" vs "touched with his foot".** Only account C has a kick (*abhyahanat*). With A or B, say "put his foot on Agastya's head" / "touched… with his foot". The series plan's "until he kicked a sage" is account C.
3. **Agastya curses (A, B); Bhrigu curses (C).** Don't mix.
4. **Shachi's request is a trap.** The sages carry him *because* she asks, on Indra's instruction. Without this beat the palanquin makes no sense, and with it the story has its one clever turn. (B doesn't mention her.)
5. **The word for the snake:** *sarpa* (5.17.15), *ajagara* (3.175.1, 16), *bhujaga*. *Ajagara* is the great constricting snake, usually rendered "python"; "great snake" is safest. **E05's tease drew a hooded cobra: draw a great coiled snake here, no hood.** Spotted, turmeric-yellow, red-eyed is the text's description.
6. **The boon in heaven and the snake's boon rhyme:** his eyes took strength from anyone he saw (5.11.6; 3.178.35), and as a snake he takes strength from anyone he holds (3.176.22). A good plant and payoff.
7. **Bhima isn't weak.** He's overpowered by a boon. He isn't angry, and worries for his brothers and mother (3.176.25–38).
8. **Yudhishthira's answer** is on conduct over birth. Quote it as his answer to the snake, close to the text; no commentary.
9. **Not the Yaksha's questions.** This is a different riddle scene (the Yaksha at the lake comes later, also in Vana Parva).

## Versions
- **Scripture, critical edition:** everything in the table above, and account C.
- **Purana:** **"Sarpa, sarpa!"** Impatient, he whips Agastya shouting *sarpa, sarpa* ("move on", but also "snake"), and Agastya turns the word on him: [Devi Bhagavata, ch. 9](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/devi-bhagavata-purana/d/doc57234.html). A great detail, but **not in the epic passages read**. Use only with "in the Devi Bhagavata" / "in one telling", or in the pinned comment.
- **Modern retellings** (never as scripture): Agastya was **short**, so he walked slower than the others (from a web-search summary; the page wasn't opened); the kick with "sarpa, sarpa" told as if from the Mahabharata ([mahabharataonline](https://mahabharataonline.com/stories/mahabharata_story.php?id=19)). The short-Agastya detail is **UNSOURCED** in the texts read.
- **Not read:** other Puranic accounts; Ganguli Vana §177 (the capture; read in the CE instead).

## Hook candidates
1. **"The gods once made a human their king. It ended with him as a snake."** (5.11, 5.17) A contradiction, and true. **Recommended**, then the trailer: *a throne, a palanquin, and a riddle.*
2. **"The strongest Pandava was caught by a snake… and it was his own ancestor."** (3.175–176) Starts from Bhima, whom viewers know; needs a jump back in time.
3. **"He said 'I'm too weak to rule heaven.' Then they gave him power."** (5.11.3–8)
4. **"A queen destroyed the most powerful man in heaven with one request."** (5.15)
5. **"His curse could only be broken by an answer."** (3.176.21, 3.177.12)

## Closing the loop
- He begins by saying he's **too weak**, and ends by telling Yudhishthira that power deludes even the wise (3.178.30). That's the line to end on.
- **Link back to E03:** Nahusha is **Urvashi's grandson** (1.70.22–24). Good for the pinned comment; too much for the video.
- **Tease for E07:** Parikshit and a dead snake: "the next curse also begins with a snake". *(E07 not yet researched.)*

## Sensitivity notes
- **Shachi** is threatened by a powerful man, and her request is strategy, not flirtation. Show her composed and resolute, never alluring; say "demanded Indra's queen" and move on.
- **Brihaspati and the sages** behave well; the gods waver (they ask Brihaspati to hand her over, 5.12.12–13). Leave that out or say it fairly.
- **Agastya:** the insult has to be clear: a foot on a revered sage's head.
- **"Who is a Brahmin":** one of the epic's best-known passages on conduct and birth. It's Yudhishthira's answer; quote it simply and draw no modern comparison.
- **The snake** shouldn't be gory: coils, no bite.
