## Versions
- **Scripture (Mahabharata core text):** everything in the table. The sage is **Kindama** (किन्दम); he and his wife are **deer**; Pandu shoots **five** arrows; the curse is **death at the moment of union**, and the wife will follow him; **no brahmahatya** (he didn't know). Pandu dies with **Madri**, in **spring**, and Madri follows him onto the pyre.
- **Vulgate vs critical edition:** the curse itself (1.109) matches in both. To confirm: whether Durvasa's "foreseeing Pandu's curse" (Ganguli §111) is in the critical text. If it isn't, the script must not state it as scripture.
- **TV / pop-culture versions (never present these as scripture):**
  - Star Plus *Mahabharat* (2013) shows Pandu killing "Maharishi Kindam and his wife by mistake" ([TVDB](https://thetvdb.com/series/mahabharat-2013/episodes/8659798)).
  - One TV recap has the couple as **tigers**, not deer, with Madri urging Pandu to hunt ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Recap/MahabharataS01E09)); which serial it covers is unclear. **The epic says deer.**
  - Popular retellings often say Pandu "died of love" or "Madri tempted him". The text says the opposite: **Madri resisted**, and Kunti's accusation is Kunti's grief, not the narrator's verdict.
- **Background:** [Wikipedia: Kindama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindama) (secondary; for cross-checking only).

## Hook candidates
1. **"One arrow is why the Pandavas are sons of gods."** Without Kindama's curse, Pandu fathers his own sons: no divine births. (§118, §120–124)
2. **"The deer King Pandu shot… spoke with a human voice."** (§118)
3. **E01 link: the first god Pandu chose was Dharma**, the same god Mandavya cursed in E01. So Yudhishthira's father is the god who is also Vidura. (§122–123; E01 research)
4. **"A sage gave Kunti the cure years before the curse."** Durvasa's mantra was given foreseeing Pandu's curse (§111, vulgate; confirm in the critical edition before using).
5. **"The curse even named who would die with him."** Kindama said the wife with him would follow him, and Madri did. (§118, §125)

## Sensitivity notes (for script and storyboard)
- **The mating deer:** keep it symbolic. Two deer close together in the grass, then the arrow; no explicit pose. Say "a stag with his mate", not more.
- **Pandu's death:** the text says Madri resisted and he forced himself on her. Don't romanticise it and don't blame Madri. Show it symbolically (spring blossoms, Madri turning away, a fallen crown, the blossoms falling), and say only what's true, e.g. "One spring, he forgot the curse… and it took him."
- **Madri's death on the pyre:** show it with restraint (flames as a symbol, no body); say she chose to follow him.
- Kindama is a Brahmana sage: shown with dignity, including in deer form (glowing eyes, a human cry).
